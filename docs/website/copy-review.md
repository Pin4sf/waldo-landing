## The short version

You're right. The new copy is accurate, but it **explains**, and the Codex copy **talked**. Five things went wrong:

1. **It reads like a spec.** Every section has a title, a subtitle, a cute line and three columns, so nothing stands out.
2. **Abstract instead of a moment you can picture.** "Waldo carries outcomes" versus Codex's "Hard meeting moves. Done before you're up."
3. **Too many cute closing lines, and some repeat.** "It goes where your day already is" appears twice, "Good dogs share" twice. About half of them don't land.
4. **The best lines are buried.** "We were promised assistants. We got a job managing them" sits in paragraph 9 of Why Waldo. It should be on Home.
5. **The story changes halfway down Home.** The hero is about agents, but the close still says "Your watch has been waiting for this" (left over from the health-first version).

The fix is to keep the new structure and the honest claims, and bring back Codex's voice: short, confident lines about a moment you can picture.

## Home

### Announcement strip
**Now:** "Kennel for Mac is in open beta. Waldo's first surface, for running many agents and getting one outcome."
**Problem:** "Surface" is our internal word, not a word a visitor would use.
- ★ A. **Kennel for Mac is in open beta.** Run all your coding agents toward one finished result.
- B. **Kennel for Mac is in open beta.** Many agents. One outcome.

### Hero
**Now:** "You can't keep up / with everything. Waldo can." + "More now gets made for you, and about you, than anyone could read. Waldo keeps up with all of it (your work, your tools, your agents, your body)…" + "Only the good bits."
**Problem:** The subtitle is hard to read out loud, and a list in brackets in the hero is a sign we haven't picked. "Only the good bits" doesn't say anything. After reading it, you still don't know what Waldo *is*.
- ★ A. **Life happens. / Waldo handles it.**
  Subtitle: One personal agent across your work and your life. It knows how you're doing, brings in the right tools and agents, and stays on it until it's actually done.
  Body: You'll hear from it when it matters.
- B. **One agent that knows you. / And runs the rest.** (same subtitle)
- C. **Tell Waldo what you want. / It handles the rest.** (same subtitle)

  Subtitle: More apps, more agents, more data about you. All of it still waits on you to read it, brief it, check it and decide.
  *(Why Waldo keeps its own version of this line. Home uses this one.)*

### Agents vs Waldo
**Now:** "Agents do tasks. / Waldo carries outcomes." + "You say what you want done. Waldo brings in the right tools and agents, and stays with it until it's real."
**Problem:** The title is good. "Carries outcomes" is abstract, so the subtitle needs a picture. The column names ("The best tool for every job", "Outcomes, not tasks", "It knows you") are labels, not promises.
- ★ Keep the title. New subtitle: **An agent can finish the code while the release is still blocked. Waldo stays on it until the release ships, and only pulls you in when it's your call.**
- Alternative subtitle (Codex): Waldo reads, Waldo does, Waldo closes.

### Who it's for
**Now:** "No two engineers / live the same life." + Engineer A / Engineer B.
**Problem:** Clever idea, but "Engineer A" reads like a placeholder, and the "Waldo:" answers are flat.
- ★ A. **Same Waldo. / Different hats.** (Codex)
  Subtitle: Starting with founders, engineers and investors, the people already running several agents at once.
  Three cards:
  - **Founders.** Three calls back to back, then the co-founder sync. Waldo puts ten minutes of air before it, so the snappy reply never happens.
  - **Engineers.** Waldo finds the hour you're sharpest and gives it to the hard problem. Standup moves somewhere else.
  - **Investors.** Pitches spaced to what you can actually give. The founder at pitch five gets your pitch-one attention.
- B. Keep "No two engineers live the same life," but give the two engineers names and a real line each: *"Priya: three agents running, toddler up at 5am. Waldo moved deep work to 2pm, after the nap window."*

- ★ A. **Where's Waldo? / Wherever you are.**

**Now:** "You're not the first. / You're also not too late. Yet." + "Your watch has been waiting for this."
**Problem:** This is left over from the health-first story. A visitor who read the agent story above hits a line about their watch.

## Why Waldo

The letter keeps the Codex thesis, and that's the right call. Three fixes:

1. **Use the best line as a pull quote.** Swap the "Done isn't done" pull quote ("Agents execute tasks. The person still carries the outcome.") for **"We were promised assistants. We got a job managing them."**
2. **Shorten the eight-verb list** ("explains… coordinates… follows… reviews… decides… checks… chases… remembers"). Keep five: *explains the goal, follows each run, checks what actually changed, chases what failed, remembers what's unfinished.*
3. **Bring back Codex's thesis sentence** near the top of "What Waldo is": *"As AI gets more capable, people shouldn't have to become managers of AI."*

The biggest gap is still **your own opening story** (the dashed box). Without it, the letter reads like a manifesto, not a founder's note. Nobody else can write that part.

## Site-wide rules for the next pass

1. **Cut about half the cute closing lines.** Keep one only if it adds a feeling the title didn't. Keepers: "On a leash you hold," "A kennel, not a cloud," "Your keys. Your call," "About time," "The dog doesn't answer these. We do."
2. **One home per line.** "It goes where your day already is" appears twice, "Good dogs share" twice, and "One Waldo" in four headlines. Pick one place for each.
3. **"Carry" appears too often** (carrying, carries outcomes, less to carry, what Waldo is carrying). Keep it in two places at most: Home's "carrying what matters" and Kennel's "Less for you to carry."
4. **No brackets or lists in a headline or subtitle.** If there's a list, we haven't picked.
5. **Every section needs one moment you can picture:** a time, a meeting, a person. "Moved your 9am to 10:30" beats "manages your schedule."
6. **Decide on "he" or "it."** Codex used "he" (warmer, and it gave us "Longer he learns…"). The new pages use "it." Pick one for the whole site. This is still an open question in [README.md](README.md).
