## Part 1 — Why the current copy is weak

"Co-ordinating your data with AI shouldn't be your job" is about a chore. It's abstract, it leads with "AI" (which our rules say is the world Waldo lives in, not the pitch), and it never says what Waldo *is*. It also reads like a complaint about tools, not a feeling a person has.

The real problem is simpler: **everything knows a slice of you, and nothing knows you.** Your watch knows your body. Your calendar knows your week. Each tool knows a task. You're the only place it all meets.

That also sets up the next section ("Agents do tasks. Waldo carries outcomes") instead of repeating it.

## Part 2 — Three angles (★ = recommended)

### ★ A. Everything knows a slice. / Nothing knows you.
**No body line (2026-10-03, Suyash).** The subhead is cut because it softens the problem. The headline and the three cards now carry it alone, so the cards are written to hit harder (below). Headline alternatives that work without a subhead:
- *It all knows something. / You do the connecting.*
- *You're the only thing / holding it together.* (bluntest)

- *Your watch knows. Your calendar doesn't.* You slept five hours and your stress is up. The calendar still has four meetings before noon, and you're the one who notices.
- *Every tool knows a slice.* Each new one wants your life story first. You spend an hour teaching it who you are, then the next one shows up.
- *Every agent knows the task. None know the why.* Every re-brief, every review and every "what did you mean?" runs through you. [This is where Kennel starts →](/kennel)

Each card now ends on **you** doing the work. That's the problem, stated three ways, with no subhead needed.

Why it works: it's a feeling people already have, it uses all three existing pictures unchanged, and the answer ("one thing that knows you") is Waldo.

### B. You're the integration layer.
Body: Every tool, agent and sensor you own reports to one place: you.

Sharper and funnier, but "integration layer" is engineer talk. Good for the Engineers hat, wrong for the whole page.

### C. Ten tools. Three agents. / One person holding it together.
Body: More gets made for you, and about you, than anyone could keep track of.

Warmest, but the least specific. Closest to the old hero ("You can't keep up with everything").

## Part 3 — Features to highlight (the Linear move)

Linear's home page has a block where each feature gets a small label, a picture, a short title and one line. No paragraphs. We do the same, once, between section 4 and "Same Waldo, different hats".

Rules it follows:
- Only things that exist today or are marked "Coming next" (from [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md)).
- None of these repeat what the home page already shows: autonomy, privacy, connectors, hats, Kennel.
- No banned words, no health outcome promises.

### Picture notes
Each tile is a small product moment drawn in code, not a screenshot: a Brief notification, a Slack message for the Fetch, a calendar diff for the Adjustment. Same card style as the "What you see of it" carousel, so it reads as one product.

### Layout
Linear-style 4×2 grid on desktop (small mono label on top, picture, title, one grey line). Two columns on phones. "Coming next" tiles get a quiet tag, no colour.
